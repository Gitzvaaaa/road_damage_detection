2.5. Representative CNN Architectures
2.5.1. LeNet
LeNet-5 is an early CNN designed for handwritten character recognition. It processes a 32 × 32
grayscale input through convolution, subsampling, and classification stages. Its C1 layer produces 
six feature maps, S2 reduces their spatial resolution, C3 produces 16 feature maps, and S4 
performs further subsampling. C5 combines the remaining spatial features, followed by the F6 layer 
and the output stage [8].
The original architecture uses sigmoid-type nonlinearities and trainable subsampling, with a radial￾basis output layer. Modern teaching implementations often replace these details with ReLU, ordinary 
pooling, and a softmax classifier. LeNet illustrates the central CNN pattern of extracting local 
features, reducing spatial resolution, and combining the resulting representations for classification 
[8].
2.5.2. VGG
VGG uses repeated 3 × 3 convolutions with stride 1 and padding that preserves spatial size within 
each block. ReLU follows the convolutions, while 2 × 2 max pooling with stride 2 reduces resolution 
between blocks. The number of feature channels increases as spatial resolution decreases. VGG-16 
has 13 convolutional and three fully connected weight layers; VGG-19 has 16 convolutional and 
three fully connected weight layers [9].
Stacking small kernels expands the effective receptive field while introducing additional nonlinear 
transformations. VGG provides a regular architecture that is straightforward to describe and adapt, 
although its original fully connected classifier accounts for a large share of its parameters and 
memory requirements [9].
2.5.3. ResNet
ResNet introduces residual blocks to ease the optimization of deep networks. A block learns a 
residual transformation 𝐹(𝑥; 𝑊) and adds a shortcut from its input [10]:
𝑦 = 𝐹(𝑥; 𝑊) + 𝑥
When the spatial dimensions or channel counts differ, a projection 𝑊𝑠
, commonly implemented by a 
1 × 1 convolution with a suitable stride, matches the shortcut to the residual output:
𝑦 = 𝐹(𝑥; 𝑊) + 𝑊𝑠𝑥
The addition provides a direct path for information and gradients, allowing the block to learn a 
change to its input representation. ResNet-18 and ResNet-34 use basic blocks with two 3 × 3
convolutions; deeper variants such as ResNet-50 use bottleneck blocks with 1 × 1, 3 × 3, and 1 × 1
convolutions. The original networks finish with global average pooling and a fully connected 
classifier [10].
