2.5.3. ResNet
ResNet introduces residual blocks to ease the optimization of deep networks. A block learns a 
residual transformation 𝐹(𝐱; 𝑊) and adds a shortcut from its input [10]:
𝐲 = 𝐹(𝐱; 𝑊) + 𝐱
When the spatial dimensions or channel counts differ, a projection 𝑊𝑠
, commonly implemented by a 
1 × 1 convolution with a suitable stride, matches the shortcut to the residual output:
𝐲 = 𝐹(𝐱; 𝑊) + 𝑊𝑠𝐱
The addition provides a direct path for information and gradients, allowing the block to learn a 
change to its input representation. ResNet-18 and ResNet-34 use basic blocks with two 3 × 3
convolutions; deeper variants such as ResNet-50 use bottleneck blocks with 1 × 1, 3 × 3, and 1 × 1
convolutions. The original networks finish with global average pooling and a fully connected 
classifier [10].
2.6. Techniques for Improving CNN Performance
2.6.1. Transfer Learning
Transfer learning builds a classifier from a CNN pretrained on a source dataset, such as ImageNet. 
The pretrained feature extractor is retained, and its original classification layer is replaced by a new 
head with one output per target class. For this four-class task, the new head therefore has four logits 
[11].
In a feature-extraction setup, the backbone weights are frozen and only the new head is trained. For 
fine-tuning, selected backbone layers or the full backbone are unfrozen and updated, usually with a 
smaller learning rate than the newly initialized head. Input preprocessing must match the pretrained 
weights, and the amount of fine-tuning is selected using validation performance. If the backbone is 
intended to remain fixed, its BN running statistics must also remain fixed [11].
2.6.2. Data Augmentation
Data augmentation generates varied training inputs through transformations intended to preserve 
class identity. Random flips, modest geometric changes, and photometric changes expose the 
model to variations that may occur in unseen images. Transformations must remain appropriate to 
the task; for example, large rotations can change the directional meaning of a road crack [1, Sec. 
7.4].
The project applies online horizontal flipping, small affine transformations, color jitter, occasional 
perspective distortion, and Gaussian blur before tensor conversion and normalization. Augmentation 
is restricted to training images. Validation and test images receive only tensor conversion and 
normalization, using statistics computed from the training set. This preserves a consistent evaluation 
procedure and avoids using validation or test statistics to prepare the training inputs [12].
