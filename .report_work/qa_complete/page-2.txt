ReLU is inexpensive and has derivative 1 for positive inputs and 0 for negative inputs. It avoids 
saturation on the positive side, although units that remain inactive can receive zero gradients. 
Sigmoid maps values to (0, 1), and tanh maps them to (−1,1); both can produce small gradients 
when the magnitude of their input is large. The project uses ReLU in its hidden layers [12].

Max pooling retains the strongest response in a region, whereas average pooling summarizes all 
responses in that region. Pooling can reduce sensitivity to small local shifts, but it also discards 
spatial detail. Global average pooling reduces each entire feature map to one value; adaptive 
average pooling can instead produce a specified spatial grid [2, Sec. 7.5].
